*Research base for "Garbha Gyan: A 40-Week Journey of Mind, Body & Spirit" (working title).*
*Compiled 5 October 2026. Status: **draft for expert review**.*

---

## 0. Method, scope and how to read these notes

**How the research was done.** Web search was used to locate and cross-check sources. Direct fetching of
full-text pages (PubMed Central, government PDFs, Sanskrit e-text sites) was **blocked by this build
environment's network policy**, so most items were confirmed from search-result abstracts, publisher
landing pages and secondary summaries. Classical-text verse numbers come from standard editions known to the
compiler and are cross-checked against secondary literature where possible. Anything that could not be confirmed
is marked **`[needs verification]`**.

**Confidence scale used throughout**

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **H** | High: guideline from a major body, or consistent RCT/meta-analysis evidence |
| **M** | Moderate: some RCTs or meta-analyses, but small, varied or of low GRADE quality |
| **L** | Low: pilot studies, single small studies, physiological plausibility only |
| **T** | Tradition: textual or cultural source; not a claim of medical effect |

**Evidence badges used in the booklet** (as specified in the brief)

- ● **Science-backed**: nutrition, sleep, stress reduction, safe movement, talking/reading/singing to baby, avoiding harms
- ◐ **Emerging evidence**: music, mantra/chant and meditation for the mother's calm, bonding rituals
- ✦ **Tradition & culture**: garbha sanskar practices, yantras, chakra frameworks, rituals. Presented as heritage
  and contemplative practice with the source cited, **not** as a proven medical effect.

**An important nuance for "●" activities about the baby.** It is well established that the fetus *hears*, *learns*
and *remembers* sounds such as the mother's voice before birth (§1.1). It is **not** established that reading,
talking or singing during pregnancy improves a child's later intelligence, language or personality. The booklet
must say "your baby can hear and come to know your voice" and must not say "this boosts brain development".

## 1. Modern science

### 1.1 Fetal hearing and learning of sounds

| Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| The first behavioural responses to sound appear at **~19 weeks**, to a 500 Hz tone. The frequency range widens first downwards, then upwards. At **27 weeks**, 96% of fetuses responded to 250/500 Hz tones and none to 1000/3000 Hz. The loudness needed to get a response falls 20–30 dB as the fetus matures. | Hepper PG, Shahidullah BS. *Development of fetal hearing.* Arch Dis Child Fetal Neonatal Ed 1994;71(2):F81–F87. PMID 7979483. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7979483/ | H |
| The womb mainly passes **low frequencies**, which carry the melody and rhythm of speech. The mother's own voice also reaches the baby through her body (bone conduction), so it is the most prominent voice. | Standard review consensus (e.g., Querleu D et al. 1988; Gerhardt KJ & Abrams RM 2000) `[needs verification of exact citations]` | M |
| **Newborns prefer their mother's voice** to a stranger's, within days of birth. | DeCasper AJ, Fifer WP. *Of human bonding: newborns prefer their mothers' voices.* Science 1980;208:1174–1176. | H |
| Mothers read a passage (*The Cat in the Hat*) aloud daily in the last ~6 weeks. Newborns then preferred **that passage's rhythm**, even when a stranger read it. | DeCasper AJ, Spence MJ. *Prenatal maternal speech influences newborns' perception of speech sounds.* Infant Behav Dev 1986;9:133–150. | H (replicated in principle; small n) |
| Term fetuses' heart rate **rose to a recording of the mother's voice** and fell to a stranger's (n=60). | Kisilevsky BS et al. *Effects of experience on fetal voice recognition.* Psychol Sci 2003;14(3):220–224. PMID 12741744. | H |
| A pseudo-word ("tatata") played in the third trimester produced **measurable neural memory traces** in newborns. The effect grew with the amount of exposure. | Partanen E et al. *Learning-induced neural plasticity of speech processing before birth.* PNAS 2013;110(37):15145–15150. PMID 23980148. | H |
| Newborns recognised a melody (lullaby) heard before birth `[needs verification]`. | Partanen E et al. *Prenatal music exposure induces long-term neural effects.* PLoS One 2013;8(10):e78946; Granier-Deferre C et al. 2011, Infancy `[needs verification]` | M |

**Booklet implications**
- **From week 18–20**, introduce "talk to your baby" prompts. **From week 24**, make them a regular weekly feature.
  **From week 30**, add the "same story, same song" ritual (DeCasper & Spence; Partanen).
- Keep music at comfortable volume and **never put headphones on the belly**. The womb already amplifies low
  frequencies, and evidence on loud abdominal sound is limited, so use caution `[needs verification: ACOG/AAP noise guidance]`.
- Honest note to print: *"Your baby can hear you and may come to know your voice and songs. This is about
  familiarity and comfort, not about making a baby 'smarter'."*

### 1.2 Touch, bonding and the fetus's responses

| Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Fetuses (21–33 wk, n=23, 4D ultrasound) made **more arm, head and mouth movements when the mother touched her abdomen**, and fewer arm/head movements when she spoke. Third-trimester fetuses showed more self-regulatory responses. | Marx V, Nagy E. *Fetal behavioural responses to maternal voice and touch.* PLoS One 2015;10(6):e0129118. PMC4460088. | M (small n) |
| Maternal–fetal attachment (thinking about, talking to and imagining the baby) is linked with postnatal bonding. Interventions to raise it are mostly small studies. | Reviews of maternal–fetal attachment, e.g., Alhusen JL 2008, JOGNN `[needs verification]` | M |

### 1.3 Taste and flavour learning

| Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Flavours from the mother's diet reach the amniotic fluid. Babies whose mothers drank **carrot juice** in late pregnancy showed fewer negative faces to carrot-flavoured cereal at weaning. | Mennella JA, Jagnow CP, Beauchamp GK. *Prenatal and postnatal flavor learning by human infants.* Pediatrics 2001;107(6):e88. | M–H |

**Booklet implication.** "NOURISH" pages can note that a varied home-cooked diet (*jeera, haldi, curry leaves,
seasonal vegetables*) gives baby early tastes of the family's food. This is a **familiarity** claim, not a
health claim.

### 1.4 Maternal stress, anxiety and depression

| Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Prenatal stress, anxiety or depression is **associated with** a higher risk of emotional, attention and cognitive difficulties in children. The risk may be up to roughly double for some outcomes. Most children are unaffected. Effects are probabilistic and are moderated by postnatal care. Reducing maternal distress is recommended. | Glover V. *Maternal depression, anxiety and stress during pregnancy and child outcome; what needs to be done.* Best Pract Res Clin Obstet Gynaecol 2014;28(1):25–35. | H (association); M (causal pathways) |
| Narrative review of mechanisms (HPA axis, cortisol, placenta, epigenetics) and outcomes. | Van den Bergh BRH et al. *Prenatal developmental origins of behavior and mental health: the influence of maternal stress in pregnancy.* Neurosci Biobehav Rev 2020;117:26–64. | H (review) |
| About 1 in 5 Indian women experience perinatal depression in pooled estimates. Screening and support are recommended. | Pooled Indian estimates, e.g., Upadhyay RP et al. 2017 Bull WHO `[needs verification]` | M |

**Booklet implications (tone is critical)**
- Present calm as **care for the mother herself**, never as blame. Print a "No guilt" note: *"Everyone feels stressed
  sometimes. Ordinary ups and downs do not harm your baby. What helps is support, rest and talking about it."*
- Mood tracker: "If you feel low, hopeless or anxious most days for **2 weeks or more**, or have thoughts of
  harming yourself, tell your doctor. In India you can also call **Tele-MANAS 14416** (national tele-mental-health
  helpline) `[needs verification of number before print]`."

### 1.5 Sleep

| Finding | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|

Waking on the back is not a cause for alarm. | Heazell A et al. *Association between maternal sleep practices and late stillbirth (MiNESS).* BJOG 2018;125:254–262; Cronin RS et al. *An individual participant data meta-analysis of maternal going-to-sleep position…* EClinicalMedicine 2019;10:49–57. | H |

**Food safety (●, high confidence)**
- Avoid **alcohol** (no safe amount; WHO, ACOG) and **tobacco/gutka** in any form, including second-hand smoke.
- **Caffeine under 200 mg/day** (about one mug of brewed coffee or 2–3 cups of tea). ACOG Committee Opinion 462
  (2010, reaffirmed). PMID 20664420.
- Only **pasteurised or boiled milk** and paneer from a trusted source. Fully cook eggs, meat and fish. Wash fruits
  and vegetables. Avoid street foods made with uncooked water or ice, and leftover food kept at room temperature
  (Listeria, Salmonella, Toxoplasma, hepatitis A/E). FSSAI consumer guidance; WHO Five Keys to Safer Food.
- **Fish**: choose low-mercury fish 2–3 times a week. Avoid shark, swordfish, king mackerel (*surmai*: check local
  advice) and big-eye tuna. FDA/EPA *Advice about Eating Fish*, 2017, updated 2021.
  `[needs verification: Indian species mapping, especially surmai/king mackerel]`
- **Unripe/semi-ripe papaya** (latex) and **pineapple in large amounts** are traditionally avoided in India. Evidence is
  limited for normal food amounts. The booklet says *"many families avoid… if unsure, ask your doctor"*
  `[needs verification]`. Ripe papaya in normal amounts is generally considered safe.
- **No herbal or Ayurvedic preparations, *kadhas*, *rasayanas* or supplements** without a doctor's advice. This
  booklet does not prescribe any.

**Nausea**: small, frequent meals; dry snacks on waking; ginger in food. ICMR-NIN 2024 recommends small frequent meals.
Severe vomiting (cannot keep fluids down) is a reason to call the doctor (hyperemesis).

### 1.7 Safe movement and exercise

| Guidance | Source | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| In uncomplicated pregnancy, physical activity is **safe and desirable**. Aim for **at least 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity** activity plus muscle-strengthening. Benefits include less gestational diabetes, pre-eclampsia, excess weight gain and postpartum depression. | WHO *Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour*, 2020 (Bull FC et al. BJSM 2020;54:1451–1462) | H |

Obstet Gynecol 2020;135:e178–e188 | H |
| **Stop exercising and call your doctor** for: vaginal bleeding; regular painful contractions; fluid leaking; breathlessness before exertion; dizziness; headache; chest pain; muscle weakness affecting balance; calf pain or swelling. | ACOG CO 804 (warning-signs list) | H |
| Contraindications include certain heart/lung disease, cervical insufficiency/cerclage, multiple pregnancy at risk of preterm labour, persistent bleeding, placenta praevia after 26 weeks, pre-eclampsia, ruptured membranes, preterm labour and severe anaemia. The doctor decides. | ACOG CO 804; Canadian guideline: Mottola MF et al. BJSM 2018;52:1339–1346 | H |
| **India:** FOGSI has promoted antenatal yoga (e.g., the *"Adbhut Matrutva"* initiative). A specific FOGSI exercise guideline document could not be located. | FOGSI Focus *Adbhut Matrutva* (fogsi.org PDF) `[needs verification]` | L |
| Pelvic-floor muscle training in pregnancy reduces urinary incontinence in late pregnancy and postpartum. | Woodley SJ et al. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2020;5:CD007471 | H |

**Booklet rules derived from this**
- Every BODY activity carries **"Check with your doctor first"** and **"Stop if…"** with the ACOG list in short form.
- **No** inversions, deep backbends, closed twists, lying on the belly, strong abdominal work, jumping, hot rooms,
  breath retention, or holding poses to the point of strain.
- **After the first trimester:** no lying flat on the back. Use side-lying or propped-up relaxation instead.
- **Balance:** standing balance poses are done only **with a wall or chair**.

### 1.8 Yoga, mindfulness and music in pregnancy: what the trials show

| Topic | Key evidence | Quality | Badge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Prenatal yoga** (meta-analysis, 29 studies, 2,217 women) | Reduced anxiety, depression and perceived stress. Associated with shorter labour and more normal vaginal births. | **Low to very low** (GRADE). Corrigan L et al. BMC Pregnancy Childbirth 2022;22:250. PMC8957136 | ● for gentle movement; ◐ for mental-health effects |
| **Safety of 26 postures** in the 3rd trimester (n=25) | Maternal vitals and fetal heart rate stayed normal. No falls or adverse events. | Small observational study. Polis RL et al. Obstet Gynecol 2015;126(6):1237–1241. PMID 26551176 | ● (safety) |

**Honest summary for the booklet:** *Gentle movement is clearly good for most pregnancies. Yoga, mindfulness and
music seem to help many mothers feel calmer. The studies are encouraging, but many are small or of mixed quality.*

### 1.9 Fetal movements ("kicks")

- First movements ("quickening") are usually felt at **16–24 weeks** (earlier in later pregnancies).
- **RCOG Green-top Guideline 57** (2011): there is **insufficient evidence for formal kick counting to a fixed
  number**. Women should learn their **baby's own pattern** and report any **reduction or change** straight
  away. Movements do **not** decrease towards term.
- The booklet's kick tracker is framed as **"Know your baby's pattern"**: a daily note of when baby is active, plus an
  optional timed log. It prints: *"If movements slow down, change or stop — do not wait until tomorrow; call your
  doctor or hospital now. Do not rely on home Dopplers or apps."*

### 1.10 Danger signs: when to call the doctor immediately

Compiled from the **MoHFW Mother & Child Protection (MCP) Card** (Revised 2018,
https://nhm.gov.in/New_Updates_2018/NHM_Components/Immunization/Guildelines_for_immunization/MCP_Card_English_version.pdf),
WHO ANC 2016 and ACOG:

1. Vaginal bleeding or spotting, at any stage
2. Fluid leaking or gushing from the vagina
3. Baby moving less, differently, or not at all (after ~24–28 weeks)
4. Severe headache, blurred vision, seeing spots, or sudden swelling of face, hands or feet
5. Fits or convulsions, or fainting
6. High fever (≥38 °C / 100.4 °F) or fever with chills
7. Severe or persistent abdominal pain, or regular tightening/contractions before 37 weeks
8. Severe vomiting (cannot keep water down) or signs of dehydration
9. Difficulty breathing, chest pain or a racing heart
10. Pain, redness or swelling in one calf
11. Burning or pain on passing urine, or foul-smelling discharge
12. Thoughts of harming yourself or the baby, or feeling unable to cope
13. Any injury, such as a fall or road accident, even if you feel fine

## 2. Classical Indian sources on *garbha vijñāna* and *garbha saṃskāra*

> **Framing for the booklet.** These texts are 2,000+ year-old works of a sophisticated medical and philosophical
> tradition. They are presented here as **heritage**: what our ancestors observed, taught and valued. They are not
> presented as modern medical instructions. Verse references follow common Indian editions (e.g., Chaukhambha). Numbering
> varies between editions.

### 2.1 Caraka Saṃhitā (c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE, redacted by Dṛḍhabala ~4th–5th c. CE)

- **Śārīrasthāna ch. 4, *Mahatī Garbhāvakrānti Śārīra***: the month-by-month formation of the embryo.
  1st month: *kalala* (jelly-like). 2nd: *ghana* (solid). 3rd: all limbs and senses appear together. 4th: the fetus becomes
  stable and the mother feels heaviness. 5th: more flesh and blood. 6th: strength and complexion. 7th: all parts complete.
  8th: *ojas* (vital essence) moves between mother and fetus. Ca. Śā. 4/9–25 `[needs verification of verse numbers]`.
  - **Dauhṛda / *dvihṛdayā*** (Ca. Śā. 4/15 ff.): from the 4th month the mother is called "two-hearted", since the
    fetal heart is said to manifest. Her **cravings should be lovingly fulfilled (within what is wholesome)**.
    *Culturally, this is the root of Maharashtra's **dohāḷe jevaṇ** (from dohada/dohāḷe = cravings).* `[verify]`
- **Śārīrasthāna ch. 8, *Jātisūtrīya Śārīra***: the regimen for the pregnant woman (*garbhiṇī paricaryā*). Ca. Śā. 8/21–22
  advises that she keep a **pleasant, cheerful mind**, receive kindness, and avoid strenuous, frightening or agitating
  experiences `[verify]`. **Ca. Śā. 8/32** gives the month-wise regimen (*māsānumāsika paricaryā*, see §4).
- Charaka lists factors harmful to the fetus (*garbhopaghātakara bhāva*, Ca. Śā. 4/18). Several are **sensible**
  (avoid alcohol, heavy exertion, fasting, sleeping on the back, suppressing natural urges). Several are **fear-based or
  inconsistent with modern evidence**, so the booklet does **not** reproduce them.
- Online reference (blocked from this environment): *Charak Samhita New Edition*, https://www.carakasamhitaonline.com
  (pages "Jatisutriya Sharira", "Mahatigarbhavakranti Sharira", "Prenatal care (garbhini paricharya)").

### 2.2 Suśruta Saṃhitā (core c. 1st millennium BCE, redacted ~3rd–4th c. CE)

- **Śārīrasthāna ch. 3, *Garbhāvakrānti Śārīra***: development month by month. The commonly quoted passage
  (Su. Śā. 3/30–31) reads approximately:
  *"caturthe … hṛdayaṃ pravyaktībhavati … pañcame manaḥ pratibuddhataraṃ bhavati, ṣaṣṭhe buddhiḥ, saptame
  sarvāṅgapratyaṅgavibhāgaḥ pravyaktataraḥ, aṣṭame 'sthirībhavati ojaḥ"*
  *"In the 4th month the heart becomes manifest … in the 5th the mind (manas) becomes more awake, in the 6th the intellect
  (buddhi), in the 7th all limbs and parts are clearly distinguished, in the 8th the ojas is unstable."*
  `[needs verification: exact wording and verse number; Nepalese recension differs, see D. Wujastyk's Suśruta Project]`
  - **Booklet use:** a lovely side-by-side with modern science. Modern embryology: the heart beats from ~week 6, but
    becomes clearly visible on ultrasound in the 4th month. Brain wiring and responsiveness rise sharply from the
    5th–6th months. Present this as **"what the sages observed / what science sees today"** and do not equate them.
- **Śārīrasthāna ch. 10, *Garbhiṇī Vyākaraṇa Śārīra***. Su. Śā. 10/3 (widely quoted):
  *"garbhiṇī prathamadivasāt prabhṛti nityaṃ prahṛṣṭā śucyalaṅkṛtā śuklavasanā śānti-maṅgala-devatā-brāhmaṇa-guru-parā
  bhavet"*: *"From the very first day, the pregnant woman should be ever joyful, clean, adorned, dressed in light
  clothes, and devoted to peace, auspicious acts, the divine, and her teachers and elders."*
  `[needs verification of exact text]`. This is a **key anchor quote** for the booklet's "calm mind" theme.
  - The same chapter contains **taboos** (e.g., avoiding looking at certain things or going to certain places). The
    booklet **omits** these, per the brief's "no fear-based taboos" rule.
  - Su. Śā. 10/4 gives a month-wise regimen (see §4).

### 2.3 Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya and Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha (Vāgbhaṭa, c. 6th–7th c. CE)

- **Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdaya, Śārīrasthāna ch. 1 (*Garbhāvakrānti*)**: conception, development and the pregnant woman's conduct.
  It summarises Caraka and Suśruta: cheerful, pleasant surroundings; nourishing, sweet and unctuous food; avoiding
  overexertion `[verify verse numbers]`.
- **Aṣṭāṅga Saṅgraha, Śārīrasthāna ch. 3**: a detailed month-wise regimen `[verify]`.

### 2.4 Kāśyapa Saṃhitā (Vṛddha Jīvakīya Tantra; pediatrics/obstetrics, c. 6th–7th c. CE as redacted)

- The classical **kaumārabhṛtya** (child-care) text. It covers conduct in pregnancy, *dohada* and newborn care. Relevant
  passages appear in **Khilasthāna** (e.g., chapters on the pregnant woman, *antarvatnī*) `[needs verification of
  chapter titles/numbers]`. The surviving text is fragmentary (Nepal manuscript, published 1938, Hemaraj Sharma).
- **Booklet use:** a short cultural note only.

### 2.5 Garbha Upaniṣad (minor Upaniṣad; No. 17 in the Muktikā canon of 108; late, probably post-Vedic)

- Describes embryonic growth: *ekarātroṣitaṃ kalalaṃ bhavati, saptarātroṣitaṃ budbudaṃ bhavati,
  ardhamāsābhyantareṇa piṇḍo bhavati, māsābhyantareṇa kaṭhino bhavati*. "After one night, *kalala*; after seven
  nights, a bubble; within a fortnight, a lump; within a month, firm." Then: 2nd month head, 3rd feet, 4th belly/hips,
  5th spine, 6th nose/eyes/ears, 7th quickened with life (*jīva*), 8th complete in all parts.
- In the 9th month the being is said to **remember past births** and resolve on liberation. This is a philosophical

### 2.9 Contemplative frameworks used in the booklet

**Pañca Kośa (five sheaths).** Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1–2.5 (Brahmānanda Vallī): *annamaya* (food/body), *prāṇamaya*
(breath/vitality), *manomaya* (mind/emotion), *vijñānamaya* (discernment/wisdom), *ānandamaya* (joy/bliss). In the Upaniṣad
this is a **teaching device**, leading attention inward step by step. The booklet uses it as a **self-care check-in**:
Body → Breath → Mind → Wisdom → Joy. This maps onto the weekly BODY / BREATH / MIND / HEART / SPIRIT structure. (T)

**Seven cakras.** The popular 7-cakra scheme (Mūlādhāra, Svādhiṣṭhāna, Maṇipūra, Anāhata, Viśuddha, Ājñā,
Sahasrāra; petals 4/6/10/12/16/2/1000; bīja syllables *laṃ vaṃ raṃ yaṃ haṃ oṃ*) comes mainly from Pūrṇānanda's
**Ṣaṭ-cakra-nirūpaṇa (1577)**, popularised by Arthur Avalon (Sir John Woodroffe), *The Serpent Power* (1919).
Earlier tantras describe **varying numbers** of centres. Modern colour associations (rainbow red→violet) are
largely **20th-century Western additions**. The traditional texts give different colours.
**Booklet:** present the cakras as a **symbolic map for emotional check-ins**. Use the traditional petal counts and the
syllable shown as script. Note that the rainbow colours are a modern convention. **Never present them as anatomy or
medicine.** (T)

**Hasta mudrās.** Hand gestures appear in ritual (*pūjā*), classical dance (*Abhinaya Darpaṇa*, *Nāṭyaśāstra*) and Buddhist
iconography (*dhyāna*, *abhaya*). The **"therapeutic mudrā" claims** (Prāṇa, Apāna, Śūnya, etc.) are largely
**20th-century popular yoga literature** with **no clinical evidence**. As a 2-minute focus for calm breathing they
are harmless and pleasant. Some popular sources advise using **Apāna mudrā only near term**. This is a traditional
caution with no physiological basis either way, and the booklet presents it as such. (T)

**Yantras & Śrī Yantra.** The Śrī Yantra (Śrī Cakra) has **nine interlocking triangles**: **four apex-up (Śiva) and
five apex-down (Śakti)**. They create **43 small triangles** in five concentric levels around the *bindu*, enclosed by
8- and 16-petal lotuses, three circles and a square outer gate (*bhūpura*). It is described in the Śrī Vidyā tradition,
e.g., Saundaryalaharī v. 11 (attributed to Śaṅkara) `[verify verse]`. Exact construction is an over-determined
geometric problem. Published constructions (e.g., Kulaichev AP 1984, *Śrīyantra and its mathematical properties*,
Indian J History of Science 19(3):279–292 `[verify]`) aim for triple-point concurrency. **Booklet code** will
solve for the triangle line positions numerically to meet the classical concurrency conditions and report the
residual error. (T)

**Kolam & rangoli.** *Pulli kolam* (dot-grid) patterns of Tamil Nadu are drawn at the threshold at dawn. They have been
studied mathematically as array grammars and closed curves (Siromoney G et al., 1974 onwards, Madras Christian College)
`[verify]`. *Rangoli* (Maharashtra/Gujarat), *alpana* (Bengal), *muggulu* (Andhra/Telangana), *mandana* (Rajasthan).
**Booklet:** dot-grid tracing pages as a mindful-attention practice (◐ for focus and calm, ✦ for heritage).

**Maṇḍalas.** In Hindu and Buddhist traditions the maṇḍala is a sacred circular diagram. Modern **colouring mandalas**
are a secular adaptation (Jung popularised mandala-drawing in Western psychology). (◐ for colouring-for-calm; T for heritage)

## 5. Mantra and sound: what the studies show

| Practice | Study | Design / quality | Finding | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Om chanting** | Kalyani BG et al. Int J Yoga 2011;4(1):3–6. PMC3099099 | fMRI pilot, n=12 (9 men), not pregnant | Limbic **deactivation** (amygdala, hippocampus, anterior cingulate) vs rest; not seen with "ssss" | L |
| **Bhrāmarī** | Kuppusamy M et al. J Tradit Complement Med 2018;8(1):11–16. PMC5755957 | Systematic review of 6 studies, **no RCTs; very low quality** | Parasympathetic shift: lower heart rate and BP; EEG changes | L |
| **Humming** (mechanism) | Weitzberg E, Lundberg JON. Am J Respir Crit Care Med 2002;166(2):144–145. PMID 12119224 | Lab, n=10 | Humming raised nasal nitric oxide **15-fold** vs quiet exhalation | M (for mechanism only) |
| **Mantra and rosary recitation** | Bernardi L et al. BMJ 2001;323:1446–1449 | Crossover, n=23 | Recitation slowed breathing to ~6/min and synchronised cardiovascular rhythms | M |
| **Mantra meditation** (general) | Lynch J et al. Eur J Integr Med 2018;23:101–108 | Systematic review, 37 studies; **>90% weak quality** | Small-to-moderate benefits for anxiety and stress | L–M |
| **Mantra meditation** (meta-analysis) | Álvarez-Pérez Y et al. Int J Environ Res Public Health 2022;19(6):3380. PMC8949812 | Meta-analysis | Small effects on anxiety, depression and stress `[verify effect sizes]` | M |
| **Gāyatrī mantra vs poem** | Pradhan B, Derle SG. Int J Yoga 2012;5(2):150–153 `[verify]` | Small, school children | Better attention-task scores after Gāyatrī vs poem chanting | L |
| **Garbha saṃskāra music or mantra in pregnancy** | No robust RCTs found | — | — | — |

**Plausible mechanism, stated honestly:** chanting and humming lengthen the exhalation and slow the breath to about
6 breaths/minute. Slow breathing is linked with parasympathetic ("rest and digest") activity. That **may** explain the
calm many mothers feel. A secular hum works by the same mechanism, which is why the "Choose your own path" alternatives
are genuine equivalents.

**Pranayama safety rules adopted** (ACOG CO 804 + yoga-therapy consensus + brief):
- ✅ Natural deep breathing; *anulom vilom* (alternate nostril) **without retention**, slow and gentle; *bhrāmarī*
  (gentle humming, no pressing on the ears if uncomfortable); *śītalī/sītkārī* only if comfortable (skip with
  cold, asthma or sensitive teeth `[verify]`); long-exhale breathing (e.g., 4-in / 6-out).
- ❌ *Kapālabhāti*, *bhastrikā*, *agnisāra*, any *kumbhaka* (breath retention), *uḍḍiyāna/mūla bandha* holds,
  forceful or rapid breathing, and breathing in heated rooms.

**On the Gāyatrī mantra:** some traditions hold that it should be learned through initiation (*upanayana/dīkṣā*),
while many others recite it openly and widely. The booklet notes this with respect and offers the *Asato mā* or
*Sarve bhavantu* prayers, and humming, as alternatives.
